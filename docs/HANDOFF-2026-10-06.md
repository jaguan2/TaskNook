## Decision logic used in this pass

These are the rules behind the edits, not just a list of their results:

1. **Gameplay scale is the source of truth.** A detail only earns space if it
   remains readable on a roughly 30–60 px resident inside a furnished room.
   Close-ups expose construction mistakes, but do not justify extra lines that
   turn into noise at the actual scale.
2. **Fix the silhouette before decorating it.** Head shape, shoulder width,
   torso taper, limb length, hair mass, and headgear footprint carry the read.
   Shading and seams come afterward and use a deliberately small detail budget.
3. **Keep one shared rig.** Front, profile, back, standing, seated, held, preset,
   and in-room residents must consume the same body/head constants and registry
   art. A screenshot-only offset or a separate preset renderer would hide drift
   instead of solving it.
4. **Use view-aware art when the object actually changes direction.** A cap bill
   and headphone cup cannot be convincingly mirrored from one front-biased
   drawing. Their front and side variants share one catalog key and scale, while
   ordinary symmetrical hats keep one reusable renderer.
5. **Presets establish style, not identity.** Complete looks may choose body,
   hair, clothes, and accessories, but always preserve the user's selected skin
   colour. Every wardrobe piece remains available to every body afterward.
6. **Prefer system fixes over one-image fixes.** The seated-sheet clipping was
   addressed in the capture helper with fixed viewport containment, scroll
   reset, bounded capture, and layout assertions. That protects future sheets
   rather than manually repairing one WebP.
7. **Use two kinds of evidence.** Automated tests pin geometry, registries, and
   state contracts; regenerated close-up, seated, and room screenshots judge
   visual balance. A pass is incomplete if only one of those layers is green.
8. **Treat packaging as part of a source change.** Any frontend/backend change
   requires the desktop executable and manifest to be rebuilt, self-tested, and
   checked against each other before handoff.

## What was completed

### 1. Compact resident proportions

The resident now follows the miniature-room reference more closely instead of
reading as a tall vector doll:

- `HEAD_SCALE` is `1.07`, applied to the complete head unit so face, hair, hats,
  and glasses stay aligned.
- Default leg height is `29`, with a supported `26–32` range.
- Shoulders sit inside the effective head width and the torso tapers without a
  pronounced triangular lower body.
- Arms use model-aware widths, a restrained bend, one depth treatment, and
  smaller tapered hands.
- The customization UI still exposes independent width, shoulder, leg, and
  torso controls. Compact, Balanced, Tall, and Broad remain starting points,
  not locked body types.

Core geometry lives in `frontend/src/lib/body.js`; rendering lives in
`frontend/src/components/character/body.jsx` and `character/index.jsx`.

### 2. Face system

- The front head outline is one continuous curved cheek-to-chin silhouette.
  Straight lower-face facets were removed because they made the face look
  triangular.
- There is no full black face outline.
- An open eye is one uninterrupted dark oval. Catchlights, sclera, pupils,
  lids, and brows are deliberately absent at this scale.
- Blush and the nose remain faint supporting cues rather than dominant marks.
- The mouth is a short, soft expression stroke.

Do not reintroduce a heavy face contour or multi-part anime eyes without first
checking the result inside an actual room at roughly 30–60 px tall.

### 3. Glasses and hair detail budget

- Glasses draw only lens rims. Bridge and temple strokes were removed because
  they read as lines across the face.
- Round frames were reduced to a `1.75` radius with a `0.5` stroke. The frame
  colour is `#51465b`, and lens fill opacity is `0.06`.
- Smooth wigs use no more than two crown-light marks.
- Curly hair uses one shadow curl and one highlight curl rather than a field of
  internal arcs.
- Hair remains built from large silhouette masses and rounded clumps; individual
  strand rendering is intentionally out of scope.

Artwork is registered in `character/glasses.jsx` and `character/hair.jsx`.

### 4. Clothing hierarchy and customization

The clothing pass removed details that survived in close-ups but became noise
at room scale:

- The puffer has one quilt seam plus its zip, instead of stacked horizontal
  highlight/shadow bands.
- A maxi skirt has one broad fold. Pleated and short skirts retain distinct,
  slightly busier fold rhythms.
- Sweater hem ribbing was reduced from six marks to four.
- The overall bib is narrower and more rounded, with smaller hardware.
- Jorts have a slight width increase to support the gardening silhouette.
- Cargo trousers use one identifying pocket rather than pocket, knee patch,
  cuff, and highlight all competing at once.
- Tank tops, cargo trousers, sandals, and the four body templates remain
  available through the real customization UI.

The complete-look presets remain Fall style girl/guy, School girl/boy, Office
wear female/male, Lofi girl, and Cozy gamer. Applying any look preserves the
skin colour the user already selected. Do not encode a skin tone into a preset.

### 5. Headgear at standing and seated scale

- Beanies, bucket hats, berets, trapper hats, and straw hats now use narrower,
  lower silhouettes so they sit on the head rather than enclosing it like a
  helmet.
- The cap uses a centred shallow visor from the front and a directional bill in
  profile. Headphones likewise use two cups from the front and one visible cup
  in profile.
- Headphone bands follow the crown closely; the compact cups stop above the jaw
  and keep the selected hair visible.
- The art-sheet fixture now renders every hat standing, in profile, and seated.
  Geometry tests pin the view-specific cap bill and compact headphone cups.

The catalog still owns one semantic hat key. Direction is a rendering concern,
not a second customization choice.

   hand-shape pass is undertaken.

   close-up, then in a furnished room at actual gameplay size.

   structure by direction, give it a view-specific renderer rather than moving
   the same front shape sideways.
